# Audience review and scoring

Use this rubric after researching the brand and before assessing the target. Adapt the questions to the medium and the audience's actual task. Keep criterion definitions stable across audiences so their results can be compared.

## Establish audience profiles

Choose a small set of materially different audience situations supported by the brief, customer research, or observed product and channel context. Each profile should specify its relationship to the brand, familiarity, goal, task or decision, point in the journey, relevant constraints, and main doubts. Cite the basis for the profile and label assumptions. Use relevant access needs when supported by the scenario. Avoid invented biographies and demographic stereotypes.

Define each profile through these reusable fields.

| Field | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Relationship | How this person encounters or participates in the brand, including people affected by it. |
| Familiarity | What they already know about the brand and the subject. |
| Intent | What they are trying to understand, feel, decide, or accomplish in this encounter. |
| Situation | Where, when, and through which medium they encounter the work. |
| Priorities and stakes | What matters to them, what could go wrong, and what evidence they need. |
| Constraints | Relevant limits on attention, time, access, language, knowledge, or ability. |

Derive concrete profiles from the audit's actual context. Consider who the work addresses, who uses or experiences it, and who is affected by it. These groups may overlap. Select profiles because their situations could lead to materially different judgments. Avoid assuming that the brand sells something or that everyone is moving toward a purchase.

Choose a manageable number of profiles that covers the meaningful differences in the reviewed work. Explain the selection and any important omissions. If the intended audience is unclear, propose profiles with the reasoning and surface that uncertainty before treating their results as representative.

Describe these reviews as simulated audience perspectives. Real interviews, observations, and analytics remain separately attributed evidence. A role-played reaction must not become a fabricated customer quotation or a claim about measured sentiment or conversion.

## Criteria

| Criterion | Questions to judge in this audience's situation | Evidence of a weak result | Evidence of a strong result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clarity | Can this person identify the offer, key message, and what to do next with their level of familiarity? | Unexplained terms, conflicting messages, or a missing next step obstruct understanding. | The relevant message and next step are understandable with the knowledge the profile has. |
| Relevance | Does the work address this person's goal, priorities, and concerns at this moment? | It emphasizes benefits unrelated to the task or omits information needed for the decision. | It addresses the task with appropriately specific benefits, information, and context. |
| Credibility | Are promises believable and supported at the level of scrutiny this person needs? | Unsupported claims, inconsistent terms, hidden conditions, or missing proof undermine trust. | Relevant evidence and clear conditions support the claims being made. |
| Brand recognition and coherence | Does the work express the identity and promise established by the research, consistently across relevant touchpoints? | Major verbal, visual, or behavioral choices conflict with established brand signals or promise. | The work expresses recognizable brand choices coherently, with variation suited to the context. |
| Voice and emotional fit | Does the language, imagery, and tone suit this person's situation while expressing the brand's character? | Humor, formality, urgency, imagery, or vocabulary clashes with the situation or brand character. | The delivery fits the situation and carries the brand's character through specific choices. |
| Experience and usability | Can this person read, navigate, or use the work to complete the relevant task, and does that experience uphold the brand's promise? | Legibility, navigation, interaction, or access barriers obstruct the task or contradict the promise. | The relevant path is understandable and usable under the profile's stated constraints. |

For a static artifact, experience can concern reading order, legibility, or finding contact details. Inspect behavior to score interactive tasks. Use recognition as an assessment of visible brand cues; actual recall by customers requires research. Score only what the evidence supports.

## Set weights before scoring

Assign each criterion a percentage for each audience, with each audience's weights totaling 100. Explain the allocations through that audience's task, stakes, familiarity, and constraints. Set these before judging the target so the weighting does not follow a desired result. Retain equal weights where priorities genuinely coincide, and explain material differences between audience profiles.

Derive weights from each profile's priorities. Someone encountering an unfamiliar subject may need more clarity. Someone facing a consequential decision may need stronger credibility. Someone trying to complete an urgent task may prioritize usability. Use these relationships only when the evidence supports them in the actual scenario. Familiarity, intent, stakes, and constraints can combine differently for each profile.

Create a weight matrix with the actual selected profiles as columns and the six criteria as rows. Supply a rationale for each profile's allocation. Use the same criteria across profiles while allowing both weights and scores to differ.

Show the profile definitions, weight matrix, and rationales in the report. If the user changes priorities, recalculate with the new weights and identify the change. A weight of zero means the criterion is outside that audience's agreed scope; explain it. Missing evidence is handled as unassessed, without assigning zero weight to conceal the gap.

## Conduct each audience review

Adopt one profile at a time. Give the reviewer its profile, task, brand evidence, target, criteria, and weights. Keep other profiles' scores out of its initial assessment so differences can emerge independently. Separate passes in one agent are sufficient. Use separate agents when the user requests them or delegation is otherwise authorized.

Walk through the work in the order that profile would encounter it. Record the specific choices that help or hinder its task. Score each criterion from 1 to 5 using the anchors below, with a cited observation and reasoning for every score.

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A fundamental conflict or barrier defeats the criterion for this scenario. |
| 2 | Major weaknesses materially hinder the scenario, with limited supporting strengths. |
| 3 | The criterion is adequately served, with meaningful strengths and weaknesses. |
| 4 | The criterion is served well, with minor weaknesses. |
| 5 | The inspected evidence strongly supports the criterion throughout the relevant scenario. |

Mark a criterion unassessed when evidence is insufficient. Record confidence separately as high, medium, or low, explaining it through source quality, inspection coverage, and profile assumptions. A favorable score can still have low confidence.

Where an explicit applicable brand requirement is violated, report that finding regardless of the weighted score. A serious access barrier or misleading claim also remains visible even if its criterion has a low weight. Avoid counting one symptom repeatedly without explaining its distinct effect on each criterion.

## Calculate and synthesize

For audience weights summing to 100, calculate the weighted score on the 1–5 scale as `sum(weight * score) / 100`. Display at most one decimal place. The result is an audit index, not a measured percentage of customers or objective brand compliance.

If any positive-weight criterion is unassessed, label the result partial and calculate it over assessed weights only. Report coverage as `sum(assessed weights)%` beside the partial score and name the missing criteria. If coverage is zero, report the result as unassessed. Do not rank partial and complete results as though they have equivalent coverage.

Keep each audience's result visible. Include a table of criteria with weights, scores, and evidence references for each audience. Explain disagreements through their goals and context, and distinguish genuine tradeoffs from evidence gaps or inconsistent scoring. Revise a score only with a stated reason.

A combined audience score requires explicit audience-importance weights. Use the user's priorities or propose weights with a rationale, clearly labeling them as assumptions. Without a basis for those weights, present the separate audience results. Broad brand adherence findings remain visible alongside audience-specific judgments.

Recommend concrete changes, identifying which audiences benefit, any audience tradeoffs, and the evidence needed to validate predicted improvements with real users. Check weight totals, arithmetic, coverage, and evidence references before delivering.
